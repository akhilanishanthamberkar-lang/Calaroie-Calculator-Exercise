MOVEMENT & WELL-BEING

Finding movement you actually enjoy

Activity can be a way to care for yourself—not a punishment or a chore. Start with what feels welcoming.

By Akhila Nishanth · A 4-minute read
↗03

If exercise has felt like something you “should” do, it may help to widen the definition of movement. A walk, dancing to a favorite song, gardening, stretching, or playing with your kids can all get you moving. The best place to start is an activity that feels accessible to you.

Let enjoyment guide the search

Try asking yourself what you liked doing before, what you’re curious about, or whether you’d rather move alone or with company. You might experiment with a few options and notice how you feel during and afterward. You don’t have to commit to a particular activity just because it works for someone else.

Make it easier to begin

Short sessions count as a way to explore. You could put on music for a few minutes, take a gentle walk around your block, or stretch while you wait for something to cook. Consider the real logistics too: time, weather, equipment, accessibility, and where you feel comfortable.

A small plan can be helpful—perhaps choosing a time or pairing movement with an existing routine—but leave room for your energy and schedule to change. A missed session is not a failure. You can begin again whenever it makes sense.

Pay attention to how your body feels

Movement should be appropriate for your current health and abilities. Adjust the pace or activity if something feels uncomfortable. If you have health concerns, pain, or questions about what’s safe for you, check with a qualified healthcare professional before making a change.

Enjoyment and consistency can grow together. Keep experimenting until you find a form of movement that feels like it belongs in your life.

A gentle prompt: What kind of movement would you choose if you didn’t have to earn or burn anything?

This article is for general educational purposes and is not individualized medical or exercise advice.
